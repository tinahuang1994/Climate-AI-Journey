# Climate AI Engineering: The Full Learning Guide

*Last updated: April 2026 | Author: Tina Huang*

---

To become a Climate AI Engineer, you must build a "T-Shaped" skill set. You cannot just learn how to prompt; you need to understand system architecture, data reliability, and user experience.

This roadmap is designed to take you from an absolute beginner to a Systems Architect through five distinct phases:

-   **Phase 1: Foundations of AI Communication**

    -   *Goal:* Stop treating AI like a search engine and start treating it like a collaborative partner.

    -   *Outcome:* Master the 5-step prompting framework and learn to control AI outputs.

-   **Phase 2: Agentic Workflows & Tooling**

    -   *Goal:* Move from "chatbots" to "agents" that have hands, memory, and specialized skills.

    -   *Outcome:* Build local AI systems that can read folders, scrape data, and collaborate in teams.

-   **Phase 3: Data Architecture & Reliability**

    -   *Goal:* Cure AI "hallucinations" (made-up facts) by giving the AI private databases and strict safety nets.

    -   *Outcome:* Build Knowledge Graphs and automated auditing systems that can be trusted with legal/climate data.

-   **Phase 4: Aesthetic Design & Usability**

    -   *Goal:* Translate complex climate data into beautiful, trustworthy, and persuasive user interfaces.

    -   *Outcome:* Master the mathematics of UI design (grids, color theory, typography, and usability heuristics).

-   **Phase 5: Model Customization**

    -   *Goal:* Go beyond prompting — learn when and how to permanently customize AI behavior through fine-tuning.

    -   *Outcome:* Understand RAG vs. fine-tuning trade-offs and apply SFT, prefix tuning, and prompt tuning to real use cases.

**🌟 Introduction: The Learning Path**

Welcome to the Climate AI Engineering guide. Learning AI is like learning to manage a team of brilliant but literal-minded interns. To make this accessible, **we will often start with casual, non-climate examples (like planning a vacation)** to explain the core mechanics. Once you understand the concept, we will apply it to one of our three **Climate Case Studies**:

1.  **Climate Watch (NDC Analyst):** Automating the extraction of methane targets from national climate plans using Terminal Agents.

2.  **The SBTi Navigator:** Building a massive "Knowledge Graph" to audit 200-page corporate sustainability reports and catch greenwashing.

3.  **Climate Triple Takes:** Designing a [beautiful digital newsletter](https://climatetripletakes.netlify.app/) that uses the mathematics of UI to frame climate data persuasively.

**Phase 1: Foundations of AI Communication**

**Module 1.1: The 5-Step Prompting Framework**

*Based on: Google's "Prompting Like a Pro" Course*

**The Concept:** Beginners treat AI like a search engine, typing short phrases like "Help with a trip" or "Summarize this climate report." Professional engineers use a repeatable formula.

**Key Learnings: The 5-Step Formula**

1.  **Task (The "What"):** Clearly state the action using verbs. (e.g., "Create a 3-day travel itinerary\...")

2.  **Context (The "Who & Why"):** Give the AI a Persona and background. (e.g., "Act as a professional travel agent. The family includes two toddlers, so the pace should be slow\...")

3.  **Reference (The "How"):** Provide examples or source material. (e.g., "Focus on Shibuya, similar to the relaxed style of this attached blog post.")

4.  **Evaluate (The "Check"):** Review the output for hallucinations (made-up facts). (e.g., You notice the AI suggested a 10:00 PM dinner, which won't work for toddlers.)

5.  **Iterate (The "Fix"):** *Never start over.* Tell the AI exactly what to fix. (e.g., "This is great, but move all dinners to 5:30 PM.")

**Pro-Tip: 4 Iteration Methods** If the AI is struggling, use these methods: Separate Tasks (break one prompt into three), Introduce Constraints ("Use no more than 200 words"), Try Different Phrasing, or Few-Shot Prompting (give the AI 2-3 examples of what you want first).

**Module 1.2: Advanced Logic & Persona Patterns**

*Based on: Vanderbilt University Prompt Engineering Masterclass*

**The Concept:** To get expert results, you must use "Patterns"---logical frameworks that structure how the AI thinks.

**Key Learnings: The Italy Trip Case Study** We use a casual example of planning a 10-day trip to Italy to understand these patterns:

-   **The Persona Pattern:** *"Act as a luxury travel concierge specializing in 'Slow Food'\..."* This forces the AI to use a specific, expert subset of its knowledge.

-   **The Flipped Interaction Pattern (The "Pro" Choice):** Instead of guessing what the AI needs, flip the script. *"I want to plan a trip to Italy. Do not give me an itinerary yet. Instead, ask me 5 questions about my budget and pace. Once I answer, build the trip."*

-   **The Semantic Filter Pattern:** *"Show me 10 things to do in Venice, but filter out anything considered a 'tourist trap' or costing over 50 Euros."* This automatically removes data based on meaning.

-   **The Fact-Check List Pattern:** *"At the end, list every opening time you mentioned and provide a warning if that info might be outdated."* This adds an immediate layer of safety to the AI's logic.

**Phase 2: Agentic Workflows & Tooling**

**Module 2.1: Terminal Agents & The Junior Developer Mindset**

*Based on: Anthropic Claude Code Intro*

**The Concept:** We are moving from Chatbots (websites where you type text) to Agents (software that has "hands" to open files on your computer).

**Key Learnings:**

-   **The Junior Developer Mindset:** Think of Claude Code as a smart junior assistant living in your Terminal. It handles repetitive grunt work (like reading 100-page Nationally Determined Contributions - NDCs) directly where your files are stored.

-   **Core Commands:**

    -   claude: Wakes up the AI.

    -   /plan: Read-only mode where Claude explains *how* it will do a task before it writes any code.

    -   /test: Verification mode. Claude checks its own work against your rules.

    -   @: Points Claude to a specific file (e.g., claude "Read \@brazil_ndc.pdf").

**Module 2.2: Sovereign Systems & Intent vs. Implementation**

*Based on: Stanford CS146S & Obsidian Workflows*

**The Concept:** "Vibe Coding" (just telling the AI to "make it better") is dangerous and leads to "AI Slop." Modern engineering separates Intent from Implementation.

**Key Learnings:**

-   **Intent vs. Implementation:** You define the "Spec" (the rules), and the Agent handles the "Implementation" (the typing).

-   **The Sovereign System:** By combining Obsidian (a private notes app) with Claude Code, you build a system where your data never touches the public cloud.

-   **The** \@Policy_Rubric**:** You create a "Gold Standard" document outlining exactly how to grade an NDC. You then use Surgical Focus to tell Claude: *"Analyze gaps in the \@Mexico_NDC_Draft using my \@Policy_Rubric."*

-   **The Context Squeezer (**/compact**):** When the AI chat gets too long and expensive, you use /compact to summarize the key decisions and wipe the messy chat history clean.

-   **The Sovereign Hand-off:** Giving the AI a mission to run while you sleep. *"I am going offline. Audit the remaining 5 PDFs in the /Inbox. If you hit a budget of \$1.00, stop."* You are scaling your *Intellect*, not your *Labor*.

**Module 2.3: Reusable Agent Skills**

*Based on: Anthropic Agent Skills*

**The Concept:** Writing long prompts every single time is inefficient. We need to build reusable "Playbooks."

**Key Learnings:**

-   **Skills Turn Generalists into Specialists:** Instead of explaining how to do data analysis every day, you package those instructions into a "Skill" (e.g., a Time Series Analysis skill).

-   **Reusable Workflows:** When the AI needs to scrape a website, it just loads your "Scraper Skill." This makes your AI workflows consistent, scalable, and modular.

**Module 2.4: Orchestrating AI Swarms (Multi-Agent Systems)**

*Based on: DeepLearning.AI CrewAI*

**The Concept:** Complex tasks require a team. A Multi-Agent system allows different specialized AIs to work together.

**Key Learnings:**

-   **The Crew:** You assign distinct Roles, Goals, and Backstories. For example, writing a climate report requires a Researcher Agent, an Outline Agent, a Writer Agent, and a Fact-Checker Agent.

-   **Processes & Guardrails:** You must limit how many steps agents can take to prevent endless loops. Collaboration between agents mirrors how real corporate teams operate.

**Phase 3: Data Architecture & Reliability**

**Module 3.1: Vector Search & Open Book Exams (RAG)**

*Based on: LangChain - Chat with Your Data*

**The Concept:** Standard AI models hallucinate because they take a "closed-book exam." RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation) forces the AI to take an "open-book exam" using your private PDFs.

**Key Learnings:**

-   **The RAG Librarian:** When you ask about "Scope 3 emissions," the RAG system finds the 5 specific pages in a 200-page PDF and hands only those pages to the AI to read.

-   **Splitting / Chunking:** AI cannot read 10,000 pages at once. We break documents into "chunks."

    -   *Pros:* Cheaper, faster, and provides traceability (you know exactly which paragraph the AI got its answer from).

    -   *Cons:* If split poorly, a sentence gets cut in half, destroying the context.

**Module 3.2: Relational Logic & Cypher Queries**

*Based on: DeepLearning.AI Knowledge Graphs*

**The Concept:** RAG is great for finding text, but terrible at mapping relationships. For the **SBTi Navigator**, we must map out complex corporate structures.

**Key Learnings:**

-   **Knowledge Graphs:** Instead of text chunks, data is stored as Nodes (e.g., "Company A") and Relationships (e.g., "OWNS", "Company B").

-   **GraphRAG:** This allows the AI to answer complex questions like: *"Which subsidiary of Company A failed their compliance audit?"* The AI writes a "Cypher Query" to trace the mathematical logic across the graph.

-   **Preventing Schema Drift:** AI loves to make up new categories. You must use Claude Code to run /test scripts to verify that the AI only creates the 5 specific Node types you allowed in your \@GRAPH_SCHEMA.md.

**Module 3.3: Adversarial Auditors & Golden Datasets**

*Based on: Quality and Safety for LLM Applications*

**The Concept:** If an AI hallucinates an emissions number, a company could be sued for greenwashing. We must build a safety net.

**Key Learnings:**

-   **The Golden Dataset:** You take 10 sample reports and manually highlight the correct answers. This is your "Ground Truth." You use the /test command to constantly grade your AI against this dataset.

-   **The Adversarial Auditor (Multi-Agent Gating):** You chain commands together. claude "Extract emissions" && claude "Verify extracted data against \@SBTi_Rules.pdf and flag discrepancies". The second AI audits the first AI before the human ever sees it.

-   **Confidence Scoring:** You force the AI to provide a score (0.0 to 1.0) and a "Reasoning Receipt" for every claim it makes.

**Phase 4: Aesthetic Design & Usability**

**Module 4.1 & 4.2: Aesthetic Math & Usability**

*Based on: MIT 6.C35 & 6.831, [climate triple takes newsletter](https://climatetripletakes.netlify.app/)*

**The Concept:** A brilliant backend is useless if the human auditor doesn't trust the interface. You cannot prompt AI for "vibes" (e.g., "Make it pretty"). You must prompt using math. We apply this to our **Climate Triple Takes** case study.

**Key Learnings:**

-   **The Visual Language of Data:** Design is the translation of data into "Visual Variables" (Position, Size, Hue).

-   **The Mathematics of Color (HSL):** Amateurs use hex codes; professionals use HSL (Hue, Saturation, Lightness).

-   **Usability Heuristics & Negative Space:** The space between text isn't empty; it's the visual silence that makes data readable.

-   **The 8-Point Grid System:** You instruct the AI: *"Make every margin, padding, and gap a multiple of 8 (8, 16, 24, 32\...)."* This mathematically ensures your interface looks like a premium, gallery-quality product rather than a cluttered amateur app.

-   **Visibility of System Status:** When a user clicks a button, the UI must react instantly (loading skeletons, color swaps) so the human brain feels safe and in control.

**Module 4.2: The Voice of Type**

*Based on: CalArts Introduction to Typography*

**The Concept:** Typography is not decoration — it is the visual voice of your product. Before a user reads a single word, the shape of the letters has already communicated a feeling.

**Key Learnings:**

-   **Type as Image:** Serif fonts (Playfair Display) communicate authority and editorial credibility. Humanist Sans (Nunito, DM Sans) communicate warmth and approachability. Monospace (DM Mono) signals raw data and transparency.

-   **The Math of Comfort:** Good leading (line spacing) is 1.5–1.6× the font size. Optimal line length is 45–75 characters. AI defaults break both rules — you must specify them explicitly.

-   **The Rule of Two:** Never use more than two font families. Pair one expressive font for headers with one neutral font for body text.

-   **Prompt for Math, not Vibes:** Instead of "make it look like a newspaper," say: "Set H1 to Playfair Display, body to Lora, limit container width to max-w-prose, and set leading to 1.6."

**Phase 5: Model Customization**

**Module 5.1: RAG vs. Fine-Tuning — Choosing the Right Architecture**

*Based on: Columbia University — Building Customized LLMs with OpenAI (Module 1)*

**The Concept:** There are three levels of customizing AI. Most companies use the wrong one.

**Key Learnings:**

-   **The Golden Rule:** RAG is for teaching the AI new *Knowledge* (your private documents). Fine-Tuning is for changing the AI's *Behavior* (its tone, format, or style).

-   **Decision Framework:** Ask one question — "Does the AI need to *know* something it doesn't know, or *act* in a way it doesn't currently act?" Know → RAG. Act → Fine-Tune.

-   **When RAG wins:** Any time the knowledge changes frequently (e.g., company policies, live data). Swapping a PDF is free. Re-training a model costs thousands.

-   **Vector Search:** The engine behind RAG. Sentences that *mean* the same thing cluster together mathematically, even if they share no words.

**Module 5.2: Fine-Tuning in Practice**

*Based on: Columbia University — Building Customized LLMs with OpenAI (Module 2)*

**The Concept:** Fine-tuning permanently rewrites an AI's neural pathways using thousands of labeled examples. Use it only when prompting fails to achieve the right behavior.

**Key Learnings:**

-   **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** You provide thousands of exact Question → Answer pairs. The AI's parameters shift to match that style. Best for: locking in a specific tone of voice or a strict output format.

-   **Prefix Tuning:** Instead of retraining the whole model, you attach a small mathematical "prefix vector" that forces the AI into a specific mode (e.g., "Customer Service Mode") without touching its weights.

-   **Prompt Tuning:** Adds a translation layer that converts messy real-world inputs into clean, standardized embeddings — so the AI always understands what the user *means*, not just what they *said*.

-   **Good fine-tuning use cases:** Mimicking a very specific tone of voice; outputting a strict proprietary format (e.g., medical billing JSON); detecting hyper-niche patterns (e.g., a specific type of cyberattack in network logs).
